# Technique cards

The seven axes the debrief rates, written in this skill's own words. The techniques are inspired by
*Never Split the Difference* (Chris Voss and Tahl Raz); nothing here quotes the book. Each card's
**Pattern** uses `<placeholders>`: it is a shape to fill from the conversation, never a line to say
as written. `scripts/report.py build` copies the cards for the two weakest axes into the report, so
keep the `## <key>:` headings.

## empathy: Tactical empathy

**What:** naming what the other side feels or faces, out loud and without judging it, so they feel
heard and relax. Includes an opening that names the negatives they might be thinking before they say
them.

**When:** early, and whenever they tense up, push back or go quiet.

**Pattern:** "It sounds like `<the pressure or concern you noticed>`." / "You're probably thinking
`<the objection they haven't voiced>`." Then stop and let them answer.

**Common mistake:** labelling your own wishes ("It seems like you'd like to pay me more") or adding
"but" straight after the label.

## mirroring: Mirroring

**What:** handing back a few of their own key words, as a gentle question, so they keep talking and
say more.

**When:** when they say something vague or loaded, and you want more without asking directly.

**Pattern:** "`<a few of their own words>`?" followed by a pause.

**Common mistake:** mirroring everything, so it sounds like an echo, or mirroring in a flat,
challenging tone.

## questions: Calibrated questions

**What:** open "how" and "what" questions that make them work on your problem and reveal their
constraints, instead of yes/no questions or "why" (which sounds accusing).

**When:** when they state a constraint, make a demand, or when you need them to solve the gap.

**Pattern:** "What would make `<their ask>` workable, given `<the constraint>`?" / "What's hardest
about `<the thing in the way>`?" / "How was `<the number or rule>` decided?"

**Common mistake:** a disguised yes/no ("Can you do better?"), or "why" questions that put them on
the defensive.

## numbers: Numbers and anchoring

**What:** letting them name a number first where you can; when you must name one, anchoring high and
precise (an odd, specific figure reads as researched); moving in shrinking steps; trading non-salary
terms instead of splitting the difference.

**When:** whenever a figure, a date or a scope count is on the table.

**Pattern:** "What range did you have in mind for `<the role or item>`?" / a precise figure
`<like 87,400 rather than 85,000>` / concessions that get smaller each time, then a non-monetary item
to close.

**Common mistake:** naming a round number first, conceding in equal or growing steps, or meeting in
the middle by reflex.

## pressure: Handling "no" and pressure

**What:** questions designed so that "no" moves things forward, comfort with silence, and holding
steady against deadlines and "this is final" without arguing.

**When:** when they apply a deadline, a take-it-or-leave-it, or when you need them to feel safe
declining.

**Pattern:** "Is there any reason we couldn't `<your proposal>`?" / "Would you object to
`<your ask>`?" / a pause after their pressure line instead of an answer.

**Common mistake:** filling silence with a concession, or answering a deadline with a counter-threat.

## discovery: Discovery

**What:** uncovering what you could not know going in: their real limits, motivations and hidden
constraints, which change the whole deal once found.

**When:** throughout, and especially before you make a concession.

**Pattern:** a label or calibrated question aimed at the thing that seems odd: "It seems like
`<the deadline or rule>` matters a lot to you." / "What happens on your side if `<this doesn't close>`?"

**Common mistake:** bargaining on the first number before learning why it is that number.

## delivery: Delivery

**What:** a calm, curious, unhurried voice; pacing and pauses; and a summary of their position so
accurate that they confirm it in their own words before you make your ask.

**When:** all the time; the summary before you make your ask.

**Pattern:** a slow, warm restatement: "So `<their goal>`, `<their constraint>`, and
`<their worry>`." Then wait.

**Common mistake:** rushing, sounding defensive, or summarising your own position instead of theirs.
